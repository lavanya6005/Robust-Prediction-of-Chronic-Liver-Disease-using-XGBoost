# Robust-Prediction-of-Chronic-Liver-Disease-using-XGBoost
The liver is of significant concern to the human body. Liver fulfils very significant roles such as production of bile and other body enzymes. One of the most
prevalent causes of death in the developed countries currently is liver diseases and it has such grim effects as making abnormal healthy functioning of the nervous
system, coughing of blood, kidney failure, liver failure and jaundice as only but a few of the diseases identified. The purpose of this research is to evaluate and
compare machine learning algorithms to evaluate the performance of the algorithms in the context of determining the best predictive performance level of a model. The
models evaluated were using ten clinical and personal factors and features such as age, gender, and different liver enzyme levels. This paper includes three different
algorithms random XGBoost, SVM, GBM and the result showed that a XGBoost algorithm produced the best results as it produces accuracy of 96%. Machine Learning is a
process that is utilised to identify patterns in big data/ large data set to make a choice, and then machines can go through a learning process.
# Model Performance Analysis
The Support Vector Machine (SVM) showed a good performance that built a benchmark against which the task was performed. Its accuracy, however, meant that it was not
very accurate in capturing all the complexity patterns of the huge 30 000 record data. As it was doing its job, it was evidently surpassed by the higher-level
ensemble approaches.
The Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) provided a big boost over the SVM. Being an effective ensemble strategy, it was much more efficient at the learning of the
imbalanced data. It had a high accuracy and had the balance of precision and the recall, which showed that it was a viable candidate. Its performance was however
totally shadowed by its more sophisticated counterpart.
Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) by far was the most effective and powerful model. It did not propose any trade-off but it rather depicted almost flawless control
of the classification task. The final overall test accuracy of the model was 99.65 on the 7,673-record test set.
